Yellow card yes, green card no

Photo: Tadeáš Bednarz / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

In grassroots football, an act of sportsmanship should count for more than a foul. Yet on the Andalusian Football Federation's website the yellow card gets a shop window and the green one only lives on the match report.

Some details say a lot about those who allow them. On the Andalusian Football Federation's website anyone can look up the yellow cards from every match, with first names and surnames. The green cards, the ones that reward fair play, appear nowhere. They are written down on the match report and die there, on a piece of paper that hardly anyone reads.

The formative years are where the footballers of tomorrow are shaped and, above all, the people of tomorrow. And there, a green card should be worth far more than a yellow one.

The reward nobody sees

The Andalusian Federation approved the green card in 2014. It was a brave and lovely idea: to recognise the child who helps an opponent up off the ground, the one who admits the ball went out off their foot or the one who apologises after a hard tackle. It is exactly what any father, mother or coach wants to see on a grassroots pitch.

That is why it hurts that, twelve years later, that recognition is still hidden away. The kid who gets a yellow finds it published. The one who receives a green card for a noble gesture does not even get the small satisfaction of showing it to their family on a phone. Without meaning to, the message that gets through is that punishment counts for more than reward.

Let the good example be seen

The solution requires no big investment or reform. All it takes is will: for the green card to appear on the website alongside the yellows and reds, and for there to be a fair-play table in the grassroots categories so that clubs and children can be proud of it.

If we truly believe that football educates, the good example has to be seen at least as much as the bad one. Yellow card yes, green card too.

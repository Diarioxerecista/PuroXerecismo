El Palmar showed us what Chapín takes away from us

Photo: Diario Xerecista

The goal celebrations at El Palmar, with players and fans separated only by a railing, raise an uncomfortable question: how much does it cost Xerez to play in a stadium with an athletics track?

Just look at the photo. The players embrace after one of the goals and, a couple of metres away, the stand celebrates with them. No track, no distance. Team and fans celebrate almost together, as if they were one and the same.

That image repeats itself every time we travel with the team: grounds where the stand is right next to the pitch and the support reaches the players. And you cannot help imagining Chapín like that: without an athletics track, with the fans at pitch level and the team truly feeling its people.

The numbers do not lie

Chapín weighs heavily. It has done so over the last three seasons, in which Xerez have taken more points away than at home. And this season is going the same way: in their five home games they have 7 points and 4 goals scored; in their five away games, 8 points and 8 goals.

The two times the team has played two home games in a row, the haul has been small: one point against Badajoz and Don Benito, and three against Lucena and Marbella. In contrast, the only time they have played two in a row away, in Tamaraceite and at El Palmar, they came back with all six points and seven goals scored.

A millstone

Many things surely play a part: the opponent, the moment, the fixture list. But it is hard to believe that the distance has nothing to do with it. A huge stadium, with the stands far from the pitch, cools the atmosphere, and the team feels it.

Chapín will be a millstone as long as it stays like this. And every trip, like yesterday's to El Palmar, reminds us of what we could have at home.

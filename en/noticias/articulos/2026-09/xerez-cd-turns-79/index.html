Xerez CD turns 79

Photo: Xerez CD

An institutional look back at almost eight decades of a club that was born in 1947 and has lived through some of the most remarkable episodes in Spanish grassroots football

Every September, Xerez memory inevitably goes back to 1947, the year in which Jerez Club Deportivo was founded, the seed of what we know today as Xerez CD. Almost eighty years later, it is only right to pause and look back at the long road travelled by a club that has experienced practically everything: historic joys, administrative relegations, forced refoundings and even the glory of stepping onto the top flight of Spanish football.

A birth marked by controversy

The club got under way competing in the Regional category, immediately achieving promotion to Tercera División. However, that first sporting triumph was tarnished by a political decision: the Ministry of Sport itself, then under the authority of General Moscardó, annulled that promotion in favour of España de Tánger, invoking a supposed "higher national interest". The blow forced the newborn club to play in the same category again the following season, this time achieving a promotion officially recognised by the Federation.

That is how the 1949/50 campaign became the club's first season in national-level football, competing among sides of the stature of Recreativo de Huelva, Real Betis and Cádiz, among others. Despite the inexperience typical of a newcomer, the Jerez side finished a notable seventh.

The fifties: first leap to Segunda and successive setbacks

The early fifties brought the club's first great historic milestone: promotion to Segunda División, sealed on a thrilling final matchday played in Tangier. The club remained in that category for five consecutive seasons, until a painful defeat in Seville, against a Betis side with nothing left to play for, brought about relegation.

The club then returned to Tercera, where it made several unsuccessful attempts to regain second-tier status, falling successively to opponents such as Badajoz and Málaga in the corresponding promotion play-offs. It was also in this decade that the Trofeo de La Vendimia was born, a summer tournament that stands as the second oldest in all of Spanish football, behind only the legendary Teresa Herrera of A Coruña.

The sixties: the name change and the return to the second tier

The following decade was dotted with successive promotion play-offs, with ties against sides such as Rayo Vallecano, in which fortune did not always favour Jerez's interests. A major administrative milestone came in 1964, when the presidency formally requested the change of name, going from Jerez Club Deportivo to the current Xerez Club Deportivo.

The long-awaited return to Segunda División would come in the 1966/67 campaign, although it lasted just one season, coinciding with a profound restructuring of the category that reduced it from two groups to a single one, causing numerous relegations, among them that of the blue-and-white side.

The seventies: Segunda B is born

The start of the decade brought a new, albeit brief, spell in Segunda División, after winning their Tercera group. The stay in the second tier lasted just one season. The most significant structural event of this period came in 1977, with the creation of a new intermediate category, Segunda División B, which the Jerez side would join after finishing among the top places in Tercera.

The eighties: a historic lock-in and a return to Segunda

This decade left two episodes of great institutional significance. The first, a sporting one, was winning the Segunda B group in the 1981/82 season. The second, of enormous social impact, took place in the 1985/86 season, when the squad staged a twenty-five-day lock-in at the Domecq stadium as a result of the delicate financial situation the club was going through. Despite that crisis, the team sealed promotion to Segunda División on the final matchday of the league.

In 1988/89 there was also a change of enormous symbolic meaning: the move from the historic Domecq stadium to the new Chapín facilities, a venue that has since become the home of xerecismo.

The nineties: institutional transformation and promotion play-offs

The last decade of the century began with another relegation to Segunda B, thus closing a particularly brilliant cycle for the club in the second tier. Shortly afterwards, the club underwent a profound legal transformation by becoming a Sociedad Anónima Deportiva. These years brought several appearances in promotion play-offs which, despite the expectations raised, did not bear fruit until the 1996/97 campaign, when the Jerez side sealed their return to Segunda after an intense play-off decided on the final matchday.

The 21st century: the historic feat of promotion to Primera

The new millennium brought the club some of the most remarkable episodes in its entire history. Circumstances beyond the pitch forced the club to play part of a season away from Jerez, first at facilities in Sanlúcar de Barrameda and later at a temporary venue, while renovation works were carried out at Chapín to host equestrian competitions.

The high point of the club's entire institutional history came in the 2008/09 campaign, when the blue-and-white side were crowned Segunda División champions, achieving promotion to Primera División for the first and only time in their history, after a demanding battle that required accumulating eighty-one points. That feat placed the club among the elite of Spanish football, although its stay in the top flight proved short-lived, with relegation confirmed on the final matchday by a minimal margin over other sides involved in the fight for survival.

The following years brought especially hard times for the club: insolvency proceedings, another relegation to Segunda B and, finally, a very harsh administrative blow in the summer of 2013, when the club was excluded from professional competition for non-payment and forced to compete in Tercera División. Added to this was the temporary loss of the Chapín stadium, handed over by municipal decision, which forced the club to play its matches at the La Granja facilities in extremely complicated sporting and financial circumstances, which would lead to yet another relegation, this time down to Primera Andaluza.

A history still being written

Almost eighty years after its foundation, Xerez CD remains a club that carries with it a history full of contrasts: moments of glory shared with an entire city, but also institutional episodes that have tested the resilience of its fans. A journey that, matchday after matchday, continues to build new chapters in the collective memory of xerecismo.

A sacrilege called Fali

Photo: Diario Xerecista

Analysis: Xerez need to break the pattern, find their best shape with a back three and wing-backs, and stop wasting on the bench a footballer who has more than enough personality and football

There are decisions which, as the matchdays go by, start to become hard to understand. And one of them, without any doubt, is keeping Fali as a luxury spectator while Xerez CD string together three consecutive matches at Chapín without finding either a win or the version of the team that was expected of them. That a footballer of his profile should still be warming up on the touchline while the blue-and-whites founder in their own backyard is beginning to be, quite simply, a footballing sacrilege.

Performances that do not match the opportunities

Xavi Molist's starting eleven is not working, and the numbers at home are the clearest proof. However, beyond the result, it is worrying to see how certain players continue to accumulate minutes and opportunities despite not offering the expected level. The blue-and-white coach faces an uncomfortable dilemma: keep faith in names who are not responding, or open the door to alternatives which, on paper and in the minutes they have played, have left better impressions.

The mirage of the back three

If there is one fact that should make the coaching staff think, it is that the team's best spells of play this season have come, precisely, when the system has shifted to a defence of three centre-backs with wing-backs occupying the flanks and link-up players operating inside as central midfielders. That shape has given more solidity, more directness and more presence in the build-up than the usual system, and it does not seem a coincidence that this tactical change has coincided with the passages of play in which Xerez have created the most danger. Perhaps it is time to consider whether that system, and not the starting one, should be the reference from the first minute.

Rubén Mesa, prisoner of his own anxiety

Then there is the case of Rubén Mesa, whose situation is beginning to be worrying beyond the strictly footballing side. The blue-and-white striker is carrying an evident anxiety to find the net again, a tension which, far from helping him, is significantly harming his decision-making and his execution of chances. It is also striking that a footballer capable of scoring 17 goals last season cannot even head the ball cleanly on the chances that come his way. Goalscoring is sometimes as mental as it is physical, and the centre-forward needs to free himself as soon as possible from that pressure he is imposing on himself.

Zequi, out of position on the left

Another matter that deserves review is where Zequi is played. The winger, playing on his weaker side out on the left, does not generate anything like the level of threat he usually offers on the right, his natural flank. His dribbling, his link-up play and his arrival in the box lose effectiveness when he is moved away from his reference side, and the team suffers from not having his best version in an area of the pitch where Xerez need someone to unbalance the game.

Corners, an outstanding task

Nor does one detail that is repeated match after match go unnoticed: at corners, there is nobody who dares to attack the near post with conviction. The absence of a close-range finish, of a player attacking the short space to surprise the opposing defence, is depriving the team of a route to goal that many sides exploit successfully and that, for the moment, the blue-and-whites do not even rehearse.

A disoriented midfield

The blue-and-white midfield, meanwhile, conveys a sense of disorientation that is hard to disguise. There is a lack of clear reference points, a lack of someone to direct the play with judgement and, above all, a lack of that touch of personality to set the tempo of the match when things get complicated.

Fali, the leadership missing on the pitch

And that is precisely where the figure of Fali comes in once again. Inside the dressing room, the defender has earned a reputation as an exemplary team-mate, one of those who never stops talking to his colleagues during the match, correcting positions, demanding and encouraging. He also possesses a footballing cunning that other members of the squad lack, that ability to read spaces and to manage the key moments that only experience and know-how can give. In a team that needs leadership, personality and cool heads in the decisive moments, it is incomprehensible that a footballer with those qualities should remain relegated to the bench while Xerez continue to struggle at Chapín.

Time is pressing and the solutions cannot keep waiting. Sunday, at the Nuevo Colombino, would be a good moment to start correcting the course.
